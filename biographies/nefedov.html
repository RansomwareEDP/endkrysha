The Protected / File 06 / Oleg Evgenievich Nefedov

The Walk-Out

Armenia arrested the head of Black Basta on an American warrant, held him for the seventy-two hours its law allows, and then watched him walk out of the courtroom during a fifteen-minute recess. Twenty days later his companies began changing hands. Eighteen months later Germany put his face on a wanted poster. His sole-trader registration in Moscow is still active.

01 / The receiptThe one case where the protection left paperwork

Every claim on this page carries one of these.

ConfirmedCredibleAnalyst inference

The other men in this series are protected in the way weather is protected: nothing happens to them, and the absence is the evidence. Oleg Nefedov is different. His protection produced documents. An Armenian prosecutor's statement, a judicial disciplinary ruling, a dissent arguing the judge should have been removed, and then, in the Russian corporate registry, a sequence of ownership changes that begins twenty days after he drove away from a courthouse. Confirmed

He is the founder and head of Black Basta, the ransomware crew that grew out of Conti's third team in April 2022 and took at least $107 million in ransoms in its first twenty months. Credible Germany's federal police named him on 15 January 2026, with a photograph, a date of birth, and a charge sheet: founding and leading a criminal organisation, extortion in an especially serious case, more than 100 German companies and about 600 worldwide. The United States has never published his name. The Treasury has never sanctioned him. Russia has never opened a case. Confirmed

The thesis of this profile is in the dates. Everything that matters happens in a window of about fifteen months, from the morning he was arrested in Yerevan to the autumn his last operating company was moved into a foundation whose founders Russian law does not disclose. Analyst inference

02 / The manFrom a cloud-mining startup in Yoshkar-Ola to the top of Conti

Oleg Evgenievich Nefedov was born on 3 June 1990 in Yoshkar-Ola, the capital of Mari El, a republic on the Volga about 850 kilometres east of Moscow. Confirmed The leaked databases that Russian journalists use for this kind of work say he probably studied at the local radio-mechanical technical college, because he was drawing income from it at seventeen. Credible His tax number carries the Yoshkar-Ola prefix, and so do the tax numbers of nearly everyone who later holds his companies for him. Confirmed That detail matters more than it looks. The network he built in Moscow and Kazan is staffed from home. Analyst inference

In January 2017 he registered BitSoft, a Yoshkar-Ola company that presented itself as a cloud-mining operation for Ethereum, Litecoin and Zcash and declared a few hundred thousand rubles of revenue a year. It was a real, small business: 5.3 million rubles of revenue and a profit in its first year, five staff, a straight co-founder from the local real-estate advertising trade who has never appeared in any cybercrime reporting. Confirmed His own declared income from it was about 60,000 rubles a year in 2017 and 2018, roughly $1,000. Within four years he owned a Porsche, a BMW X6 and a Mercedes-AMG S63, and in 2021 he picked up a speeding ticket in the S63. Credible

Three names, one habit

The reconstruction of his criminal career rests on two bodies of leaked material and one lapse in operational hygiene. In August 2020 a user called washingt0n32 registered on a Russian cybercrime forum claiming more than ten years of penetration-testing experience; by May 2021 the same person, posting as p1ja, said his access to the REvil affiliate programme had been withdrawn. From November 2021 a manager called Tramp appears in the Conti chats that were leaked the following February, already senior enough that analysts doubted it was his first handle, running what he described to a colleague as "a second panel and a second team", co-negotiating a $500,000 ransom and personally holding the wallet for another. When Conti dissolved in 2022, Tramp's team became Black Basta, and inside its own chat system he became GG. Credible

The lapse was a password. The string 123123 recurs in files attributed to him across the REvil, Conti and Egregor eras, and a Windows machine name that appears in his Black Basta chats was matched to twenty-eight LockBit victims in 2022. A phone number tied to domain registrations at a Yoshkar-Ola address shows up in a caller-ID database as "Mr Tramp". Credible When the German notice landed in January 2026 it listed, in one line, the handles that the journalists and the chat leak had spent a year stitching together: tramp, tr, gg, AA, kurva, Washingt0n, S.Jimmi. Confirmed

How he ran it

Black Basta did not run from a bedroom. From late 2023 it had two offices in Moscow, and GG moved between them with a driver, controlled who had keys, and scolded his administrators about the state of the premises. Credible He had final say on every ransom figure, set the affiliate splits, supplied the receiving addresses and distributed salaries and commissions; the German charge sheet describes the same role in its own vocabulary, choosing targets, recruiting, assigning tasks, joining negotiations, managing the proceeds. Confirmed In May 2024 he approved the attack on Ascension, the 140-hospital American health system, over the objection of his own negotiator, who told him to go to hell; afterwards he told the team he had known what he was doing. Credible

His administrators described him as driven by personal gain without regard for the team, and the people who had been with him since Conti complained about his fee arithmetic there too. By the time the internal chats leaked in February 2025 the crew had been mostly inactive for months, and the analysts who read the archive put the collapse down to one cause: him. Credible

03 / Seventy-two hoursWhat a courtroom in Yerevan saw

On the morning of Friday 21 June 2024, criminal-intelligence officers from Yerevan's Shengavit division detained a 34-year-old Russian on Amiryan Street in the city centre. The Interpol notice they were acting on was not public. It had been raised at the request of the United States, and the first Armenian report placed the underlying charges in the state of Washington. Credible Armenian law gives a prosecutor seventy-two hours to get a detained foreigner in front of a judge and obtain a pre-extradition remand. Confirmed

The prosecutor filed on Saturday at 13:30, about twenty-six hours in. Judge Artush Gabrielyan of the Yerevan court of general jurisdiction accepted the case the same day and listed the hearing for Monday at 10:00, forty-four hours after the filing and one hour before the clock ran out. At the hearing the defence lawyer, Hayk Alumyan, pointed out that the American material had not been translated into Russian and asked for a day. The judge refused the day and granted fifteen minutes, with an order that a translator be brought to the hall and that the detainee not leave it. Nefedov left it. He walked the corridors of the building with his lawyers, took a phone call, went outside, got into a car and was driven away, in view of the court police and the bailiffs. By the time the judge came down to announce a detention order there was nobody to detain. Confirmed

The Prosecutor General's Office confirmed the whole sequence in a public statement in September 2024, after the judge had argued before the Supreme Judicial Council that the prosecutors were at fault for not supplying a translation. The Council gave Gabrielyan a strict reprimand on 4 October 2024. One member, Karen Tumanyan, filed a dissent saying the conduct warranted ending his judicial career. The judge's defence was that he had a heavy docket, was not obliged to list the case earlier, and had not believed the escape was planned. Confirmed

What he said about it afterwards

His chat account went silent on 20 June and came back on 3 July. The first message was that he had returned from a place nobody comes back from. In the private conversation that followed, with the operator of the Qakbot botnet who went by Chuck, he explained how. Credible Chuck has a name now. The United States indicted Rustam Rafailevich Gallyamov of Moscow in May 2025 as the man who built and ran Qakbot from 2008, and listed "Chuck" and "Cortes" among his aliases; the chat account that received Nefedov's account of Armenia sent almost all of its 1,374 messages in a private room with him. Confirmed

Remember when I said I had friends at a really high level? This is the level of our first. I just asked for a pass. A green corridor. Our people picked me up. I've been told that number one knows me, and that without his agreement they wouldn't have done anything.

GG to Chuck, early July 2024, Black Basta internal chat as leaked in February 2025 and rendered by Trellix, Intel 471 and LeMagIT. Chuck asked whether "number one" meant Putin. GG neither confirmed nor denied it. Credible as to what was said. Unverified as to what was true.

He told Chuck that Russian law enforcement could block an Interpol request in the country holding a suspect, that he had been shown his own file "but not all of it", and that he suspected someone had informed on him for the $10 million the State Department had offered for Conti's leadership in 2022. A week later he reported that his phone had been seized in Armenia and that "they" now had total access to his Apple account. The relationship he was describing was not new. In a Tox conversation from November 2022 he had written that he had "guys from Lubyanka and the GRU" and had been feeding them for a long time. Credible

What the record does not contain is any Russian fingerprint on the Armenian side. No diplomat, no consular note, no named official. The Armenian material establishes that he drove away from the court; it does not establish how he crossed the border. One detail from the period is worth the weight of a fact: in June 2024 passport control at Yerevan's airport was still staffed by Russian border guards, a Soviet-era arrangement that Armenia ended a few weeks later. Credible A man with a "green corridor" and a Russian border post at the airport did not need anyone to break a law for him. Analyst inference

Two years on, Armenia is still executing American cyber warrants at the same airport, and when it detained a Russian tourist on one in July 2026, a man his lawyers say was mistaken for a namesake, the Russian foreign ministry intervened publicly within days. For Nefedov in 2024 there was no intervention anyone could see, and none was needed. Credible

04 / Twenty daysWhat happened to his companies after the car drove off

He came home to Moscow and did not go quiet. He went to the registry. The Russian corporate record for the six months after Yerevan reads as a man methodically taking his name off things, and the first move was made before he had even resumed posting in his own chat. The 2022 date appears to be an error. The correction moves both transfers from "ordinary family restructuring" to "response to a specific event", and that is the finding this section rests on. 05 / The roof has a balance sheetA wife, two nominees, one home town

The "personal fund" is a recent invention in Russian civil law, in force since 2022: a private foundation into which a living founder settles assets, run by a manager of the founder's choosing, with the founder and the beneficiaries left out of the public register. Confirmed It was sold to the public as an estate-planning tool for wealthy Russians cut off from Western trusts by sanctions. It is also, exactly, the instrument a man would want if he expected to be named on a wanted poster within the year. Analyst inference

Two of them appear in his orbit within six weeks of each other in the summer of 2025. Neither discloses a founder. Both are run by people who have been holding things for him for years. Confirmed

The first, registered in Moscow in May 2025, is directed by Ekaterina Shafigullina. She was the director of his Kazan restaurant company Polis from 2020 until he took the chair himself for three weeks in 2022 to wind it down; she took over Avangard Bar when he left it in July 2024; she owns the small accounting firm, registered at the Kazan lounge's address in October 2024, that does the books for the whole cluster. Her own record starts small and local: a toy-retail sole trader in Yoshkar-Ola in 2012 under her maiden name, Davidova; the Tatar surname she carries now arrived with a marriage sometime before 2020, to a husband no registry has surfaced. Her fund reported a balance sheet of 1.02 billion rubles at the end of 2025, roughly $12.5 million, the largest figure anywhere in the network. Its accounts show what that is: 786 million rubles of fixed assets and 232 million of receivables, paid in entirely as a founder's property contribution during the fund's first seven months, with no borrowing. Confirmed The fund's own website describes a single asset: the Eco Polis retail complex at Gribanovo, on the Ilyinskoye highway outside Moscow, "where shops, restaurants and service companies are already open". The restaurant company Nefedova founded in February 2026 is registered at that exact address. Credible Someone settled a shopping centre worth roughly $10 million into a foundation run by his former restaurant manager, and his wife opened a business inside it. The title deed is not public. Analyst inference

The second, registered in Odintsovo in July 2025, is directed by Ilya Buyskikh, whose tax number comes from Medvedevo, the district next to Yoshkar-Ola, where he sold groceries as a sole trader from 2018 until the end of 2024. He has been the director of record at Nefedov's Kazan parcel-delivery franchise since 2020, a real CDEK counter whose revenue fell from 8 million rubles in 2023 to 3,000 in 2025 before he was appointed to wind it up; he held the other 20 percent of Avangard Bar, registered the abortive AV Bar five days after the arrest, and in September 2025 bought into a Kazan seafood restaurant alongside a Moscow restaurateur. In March 2026 he registered himself as a real-estate broker. His fund is the one that absorbed Sistema Dela, the developer, in the autumn of 2025, and it has since founded a Kazan housing project with a 141-million-ruble balance sheet, a second developer, and a bathhouse business in Lapino, the gated stretch of the Rublyovka where Moscow's money lives. Its registered address is shared with the bathhouse. The gymnastics tournament his charity runs every May is held in the same district, sponsored by Sistema Dela. Confirmed

The wife's own footprint is the quietest of the three and the most telling. Yulia Nefedova's tax number was issued in Yoshkar-Ola; she has been a sole trader letting property since 2016, moved to the Moscow region in 2021, and had no company to her name until 18 July 2024. In February 2024, four months before Armenia, she filed a trademark through a Moscow patent bureau for a beauty-services brand, giving as her address an apartment in the Headliner towers beside Moscow City. Confirmed Russian leak-based reporting says she flew to the United States to give birth there in 2018 and again in 2021, with her mother along on the second trip, which is why the children of a man wanted by the Americans hold American papers. Credible Since July 2024 she holds the Futura lounges outright, with 198 million rubles of revenue in 2025, and has founded two more venue companies with the same partners this year. Confirmed

Nobody in this cluster appears in any cybercrime reporting under their own name. They are not affiliates. They are the people from home who sign. What the structure says is the same thing the Kovalev profile found in a different shape: the fortune is visible, it is in Russia, and it is held by people the sanctions lists have never heard of. Analyst inference

Collection gap · unresolved

Hypothesis. The 1.02-billion-ruble fund is the consolidation vehicle for Black Basta proceeds converted to rubles through Russian exchangers and real estate, and the Odintsovo fund is the operating arm that spends them on development. The leaked chats describe exactly this conversion path: bitcoin to Monero to Tether, then Russian instant exchangers and cash, with one documented 12-million-ruble cash-out in November 2023.

Where the answers sit. The two funds' charters and founder resolutions, which are filed with the tax service but not published. The accounts of the Kazan accounting firm that services the cluster. The Moscow and Kazan land registry (Rosreestr) for the Eco Polis complex at Gribanovo, the Headliner apartment, and the Lapino, Zubovsky Boulevard and Yamasheva Avenue addresses. The Tatarstan construction permits for the Severny project, which name the project's financing. The alcohol licences of Futura Bar, renewed as recently as September 2026, which require a declared beneficial owner. And the Yandex Food delivery records from the 2022 breach, which place him at specific residential addresses.

What would close it. Any one of the four exchangers that Elliptic traced Black Basta proceeds through, matched against the fund's bank. No open source gets there. A liaison request to a Russian bank does not either. This is the gap that sanctions designation of the two funds and their directors would make visible, because the funds hold real estate that cannot leave Russia and cannot be sold without a registered buyer.

06 / The moneyWhat the crew took and what he kept

Elliptic's reconstruction of Black Basta's receiving wallets found at least $107 million paid by more than ninety victims between the spring of 2022 and November 2023, with a largest single payment of $9 million and eighteen payments above $1 million. The operator's average cut was about 14 percent, affiliates kept the rest, and about a tenth of each ransom was forwarded to the Qakbot operators when Qakbot had supplied the access. Credible Millions went to Garantex, the Moscow exchange the United States sanctioned in 2022 and seized in March 2025; when the Treasury re-designated it together with Grinex, the exchange that reopened under a new name with the same operators, on 14 August 2025, it named Black Basta among the crews whose proceeds the pair had laundered. Where that cash-out moved afterwards is its own story. Confirmed

GG's own share can be glimpsed in the chats. A $1.3 million ransom paid in January 2024 came into an address under his control and a tenth of it went out to his senior administrator; a $6 million ransom in May 2024 was split with $2.4 million passed to another actor for distribution. French reporting based on the same material put his personal holdings at more than 20 bitcoin and his control, in January 2023, at about 2,000 bitcoin, which was roughly $45 million at the time. Credible

On the chain

He posted his own wallets. Twice in the Conti chats and more than forty times in the Black Basta chats, GG typed a bitcoin or Ethereum address into a conversation that later leaked, and the public blockchain records what happened to each one. Nothing below needs a vendor's clustering; it is his own words matched against a block explorer. Confirmed

On 30 November 2021 an address was posted in a Conti payment thread as "the BTC wallet for payment"; 19.54 BTC, about $1.1 million, landed in it the next day. On 18 January 2022 Tramp posted another in his own name; 24.14 BTC, about $870,000, arrived six days later. Both were emptied within days. On 12 January 2024, in the Black Basta chat, he told his negotiator "I think 1,500 and we give them the wallet", pasted an address, and said "here is the wallet for them". Two days later it received 31.2161 BTC, about $1.3 million. On 24 December 2023 he wrote "we're done with her", pasted an address and the word "20%"; it took 0.68 BTC the next day. On 14 March 2024 he posted "7.52 btc =" and an address; 7.52 BTC moved through it that day. On 26 May 2024 he pasted a ransom note line, "Our BTC wallet for payment is", with an address that received 7.86 BTC two days later. Confirmed

The one that matters most is dated 3 July 2024. That afternoon, in the private room where he had just told Chuck about Armenia and the green corridor, he pasted a fresh address and wrote "here, minus 10%". 38.20 BTC, about $2.3 million, passed through it the same day. Confirmed Nine days out of an Armenian courtroom, with his phone in police hands and his Apple account compromised, he was back at the till. Analyst inference

He also posted an Ethereum address, on 31 January 2024, as his own. The public ledger shows $16.0 million in Tether moving through it between October 2022 and 1 July 2024, in 789 transfers, with $11,000 of it going to the address Lapa had identified as his personal Tether wallet. The last outgoing transfer, $4,761, is dated 1 July 2024, a week after the walk-out. It has not moved since. Confirmed Where the $16 million went next is a question for the people with clustering tools. What the open ledger establishes is the scale, the dates, and that the man who could not be extradited was still settling accounts the week he got home. Analyst inference

07 / The posterWanted by Germany. Registered in Moscow.

The German notice is unusual in one respect. The United States had the warrant, as Yerevan proved; the United States has published nothing. The $10 million State Department reward from August 2022 names "Tramp" as an alias and no one as a person. No indictment, no FBI poster, no Treasury designation, no reward entry carries his name as of this writing. The Frankfurt prosecutors and the Bundeskriminalamt went public instead, with the European Union's most-wanted list and a public Interpol notice the same day, and coordinated raids on two hash-cracking contractors in western Ukraine the day before. Confirmed

The notice says he is in Russia and that his exact whereabouts are unknown. His sole-trader registration says Moscow, Presnensky district, and it is active. His charity lists him as director and gives a working phone number. His courier franchise entered liquidation in August 2026 with his nominee as liquidator, a procedure that requires the owner's signature. The Russian federal press has not printed his name once. The Russian security trade press relays the German notice with the word "alleged" and moves on. There is no Russian case, no Russian statement, no Russian denial. Confirmed

In the chats he said he and Chuck would keep working "as long as grandpa lives". Who grandpa is was never spelled out. The crew he led is finished, its members scattered to Cactus, Akira and BlackSuit, its leak site dark since February 2025. Credible The man is not finished. He has a developer, two foundations, a charity that delivers drones to the front, and a judge's reprimand in Yerevan as the only consequence anyone has managed to impose. Analyst inference

08 / OpenWhere this profile is thin

Unresolved

The American case. Armenia acted on a US request and placed the charges in Washington state. No charging document has surfaced. If the case is sealed, the district and the counts are unknown; if it was never a case, the Interpol notice rested on something else. Either answer changes the picture.

The release date. Armenian court records put him in the hearing on Monday 24 June. One vendor, citing leaked flight data, has him landing at Sheremetyevo on Sunday 23 June. Both cannot be right. The court record is the primary document and this profile follows it.

The border. No Armenian source documents how or where he left the country.

The parents. Nothing in the open record identifies either parent, which in a profile built on who holds what is a real hole. The surname is common enough that a name search in Yoshkar-Ola returns nothing attributable.

The brand share. He told associates he held intellectual-property rights in the hookah franchise's international venues. The Russian franchisor's register shows no trace of him. If the claim is true, the holding is outside Russia, most plausibly in the Emirates, where the chain has five venues and he reportedly bought property.

Eco Polis. The fund's website names the complex and the accounts carry the asset; the Rosreestr title is not public from outside Russia. Until it is seen, "appears to own" is the most the record supports.

The other name. In April 2025 the GangExposed leak account published a 16-page case that "Tramp" was not Nefedov at all but Oleg Vyacheslavovich Kucherov, born 1973 in the Samara region, the sanctioned TrickBot member known as "gabr", and that "Nefedov" was a cover identity. Its evidence was dialect, prison slang and a claimed age. Nine months later the German notice carried Nefedov's photograph and a 1990 date of birth. The account later published a second, different claim tying Nefedov to a new ransomware brand, then deleted both pieces. Neither is relied on here.

The second Interpol image. The notice holds a photograph that is not the one the German police published. Nobody outside law enforcement has seen it.

Sourcing and method

Claims are labelled Confirmed where they rest on a primary document (a court or prosecutorial record, a police notice, a registry entry), Credible where they rest on reputable reporting or named vendor analysis of the leaked chats, and Analyst inference where they are our reading. The registry timeline in section 04 and the foundation structure in section 05 are our own, produced by reading the Federal Tax Service's register of legal entities through Russian corporate-data mirrors, entry by entry, rather than by citation. The on-chain figures in section 06 come from matching addresses posted in the leaked Conti and Black Basta chats against the public blockchain; dollar values are rough conversions at the date of each transfer and are not comparable across years, which is why the bitcoin amounts lead. The wife and the two associates are named because they are the registered holders and officers of the companies this profile is about; no tax number, address or other personal identifier of theirs is printed. Minor children appear as a fact only.
